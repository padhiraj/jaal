Part A — Explain it to anyone

A1. The one-line pitch

JAAL reads the scattered records police already have (FIRs, phone records, bank transfers, vehicle cameras, social media), joins them into a single map of "who is connected to whom", and points to the people quietly running the network, with proof for every claim.

A2. The story (use this with non-technical listeners)

Picture three police stations in three states. Mumbai has a cyber-fraud case: callers pretended to be bank officers and stole money. Hyderabad seized 42 kg of ganja from a van. Kolkata's bank reported a young man whose account received money from strangers and immediately sent it on. Each station sees only its own case. Each arrests a few small players. The cases look unrelated.

Behind all three is one man. His name is in no FIR. He never calls the small players. He speaks only to three trusted people (two "lieutenants" and an accountant), and only on cheap SIM cards registered in fake names, which he throws away every month. His money comes back to him through companies run by his accountant.

The clues are there, just in different places. The phone company knows which SIMs were used in which handset. The bank knows where money went. The toll cameras know which vehicles travelled together at 3 a.m. A human cannot cross-check thousands of rows across five systems. A computer can.

JAAL does exactly that:

  1. It reads everything, including the free-text FIRs.
  2. It works out who is who. "Imraan Shaikh @ Bhaijaan" in the FIR, "IMRAN SHEIKH" in the phone records and "Md. Imran Sheikh" at the bank are one man.
  3. It draws one big map. People are dots, and calls, money, shared vehicles and messages are lines.
  4. It asks smart questions of the map. Who sits in the middle of everything? Who would we remove to break the network? Who is never named but is surrounded by people who are? Which "anonymous" SIM was used in the same handset as a known person's SIM?
  5. It shows its proof. Click any claim and it opens the exact line in the original phone record or FIR.

On our test data, the hidden boss ranks #2 of 81 people. Simply counting phone contacts puts him at #42. JAAL also catches his burner SIMs and links the three "unrelated" cases, while correctly leaving two genuinely unrelated cases (a bike theft and a neighbour's fight) alone.

A3. A two-minute talk (memorise the shape, not the words)

Problem (20 s). "Organised crime works as a network, but police data is split into silos: FIRs in one place, phone records in another, bank data in a third. The people at the top stay out of every FIR, so they stay invisible."

What we built (30 s). "JAAL takes all these sources, resolves different spellings and numbers into real people, and builds one knowledge graph. Then it uses network science: who connects the groups, who would hurt the network most if arrested, who is suspiciously close to the accused yet never named."

Proof (30 s). "We planted a 12-person syndicate inside 66 ordinary civilians. JAAL puts the hidden boss at #2. Counting phone contacts puts him at #42, and puts an innocent delivery boy at #1. It attributes his two burner SIMs to him because he once put his own SIM in the burner handset, and it links the Mumbai, Hyderabad and Kolkata cases."

Trust (25 s). "Every score is broken into five named parts with a plain-English reason. Every line in the graph opens the original record. Every access is written to a tamper-evident audit log, and a constable sees masked phone numbers while an analyst sees everything."

Close (15 s). "It needs no internet and no installation beyond Python, so it runs on a police-station laptop. Paste a new FIR and in half a second it tells you which old cases it belongs to."

Part B — Concepts you must understand first

You need only these six ideas to understand all of JAAL. Each has a tiny worked example. The numbers were produced by JAAL's own code.

B1. Graphs

A graph is a set of nodes (things) joined by edges (relationships). In JAAL a node can be a person, phone, account, vehicle, case and so on, and an edge can be "called", "transferred money to", "owns", "named in" and so on. An edge can carry a weight (how many calls) and be directed (money flows A → B).

In code, JAAL stores the person graph as an adjacency dictionary: {"A": {"B": 12, "C": 3}, ...}, meaning A–B have 12 interactions and A–C have 3. A path is a chain of edges. The shortest path is the one with the fewest hops (or the lowest total cost).

Throughout Part B we use this toy network: two triangles of friends joined only through K.

B2. Entity resolution (ER)

ER decides which records describe the same real-world person. It is hard because names are spelled differently, people use aliases and initials, phone numbers are written in many formats, and some data is missing. JAAL handles it in three moves:

  1. Normalise: +91 98200 11457, 09820011457 and 919820011457 all become 9820011457. Names are lower-cased, honorifics (Md., Shri…) are removed, and common transliteration differences are folded away: "Imraan Shaikh" and "Md. Imran Sheikh" both become imran sek.
  2. Score pairs of records by adding up evidence: shared ID +1.00, shared phone +0.45, same DOB +0.35, name similarity × 0.60, same city +0.10, with penalties for conflicts.
  3. Group records whose score is ≥ 0.95 using a union-find structure, so that if A = B and B = C then A, B and C form one person. Pairs between 0.65 and 0.95 go to a human.

Jaro–Winkler similarity measures how alike two strings are, from 0 to 1. It counts matching characters that are close in position, penalises swapped letters, and gives a bonus for a shared beginning. Example: JW("martha", "marhta") = 0.961.

Worked example. FIR record "Imraan Shaikh, mob +91 98200 11457" vs. telecom record "IMRAN SHEIKH, 919820011457": shared phone +0.45, plus name similarity 1.00 × 0.60 = +0.60, giving 1.05 ≥ 0.95, so merge. Two different "Amit Kumar" records with different phones and cities score only 0.60, so they are kept apart.

B3. Centrality: "who is important in a network?"

  • Degree = number of connections. Simple, but it rewards busy people (a delivery boy) rather than important ones.
  • Betweenness = the fraction of all shortest paths between other pairs that pass through you. It finds brokers and bridges. In the toy graph, K has betweenness 0.60 even though K has only 2 connections. C and D have 0.533, and A, B, E and F have 0. JAAL uses Brandes' exact algorithm, which runs one breadth-first search per node and then accumulates "dependencies" backwards.
  • Weighted betweenness: the same, but a frequent tie counts as "shorter". JAAL's tie cost is 1 / (1 + ln(1 + w)): 1 interaction costs 0.59, 10 cost 0.29, and 100 cost 0.18.
  • Eigenvector centrality = you are important if your neighbours are important. It is computed by repeatedly giving each node the sum of its neighbours' scores and re-normalising ("power iteration").
  • Disruption (efficiency loss). Global efficiency is the average of 1/distance over all pairs (unreachable pairs count as 0). The toy graph has efficiency 0.611. Removing K drops it to 0.286, a 53% loss. This is the "whom should we arrest to break communication?" measure.

B4. PageRank and personalised PageRank (PPR)

PageRank imagines a random walker who follows edges and sometimes "teleports". Personalised PageRank always teleports back to chosen seed nodes, so the score means "how often does a walk starting from the suspects end up here?" In JAAL the seeds are everyone named as accused in an FIR, so PPR measures closeness to the case. Toy example with seed A: A 0.31, C 0.26, B 0.21, K 0.09, D 0.07, E and F 0.03. Suspicion fades with distance from the seed.

B5. Communities (Louvain)

A community is a group of nodes more connected to each other than to the rest. Modularity measures how good a split is. Louvain is a greedy algorithm: move each node to the neighbouring community that increases modularity most, repeat until nothing improves, then merge each community into a single "super-node" and repeat. On the toy graph it finds {A, B, C, K} and {D, E, F}. On the JAAL data it finds 8 communities with modularity 0.69, which roughly match the city cells.

B6. Hash chains (tamper-evident logs)

A hash (SHA-256) turns any text into a 64-character fingerprint. Change one character and the fingerprint changes completely. In a hash chain, every log entry stores the hash of the previous entry and then its own hash. If anyone edits entry #3, its hash no longer matches, and entry #4's "previous hash" no longer matches either. The verifier recomputes the chain and reports exactly where it breaks. Blockchains use the same idea, without the mining.

C1. Step 1: the synthetic data generator (jaal/datagen.py)

Why build fake data? Real FIRs, CDRs and bank data are confidential. More importantly, with fake data we know the right answer, so we can measure whether the system finds it. The generator writes a hidden ground_truth.json that the engine never reads. Only the evaluator reads it.

1.2 How the generator works

  1. Seeded randomness. random.Random(1337) makes every run produce identical data, which is essential for reproducible results.
  2. People. The 12 syndicate members get fixed phones. Civilians get random Indian names from city-specific surname lists (22 Mumbai, 12 Hyderabad, 12 Kolkata, 10 Pune, 10 Delhi). Names needed by the FIR text are reserved and never reused as random victims or family.
  3. Relationships as "call plans". Each tuple (phone_a, phone_b, rate_per_day, first_day, last_day, kind, spike_group) is a relationship. For each day the number of calls is drawn from a Poisson distribution with that rate (Knuth's method). Spike groups multiply the rate ×4 on the 2 days before each incident, so the "spike before incident" pattern exists in the data. Arrested people's plans end on their arrest day ("going dark").
  4. The slip. Two calls from the kingpin's own number are written with the burner handset's IMEI. This single detail is what lets JAAL attribute the burners.
  5. Money. Civilian background transfers are small (Rs 500–25,000). Fraud flows run victim → mule → (structured chunks) → Desai Trading → Anaya Exports → kingpin, plus the cycle Desai → Anaya → Rathore account → Desai.
  6. Vehicles. Each vehicle passes cameras in its city with 30% probability per day. On 3 chosen nights, the courier's van and L2's car pass Kurnool toll 2–8 minutes apart.
  7. FIRs are written as realistic text templates with deliberately varied phone formats, aliases ("@ Bhaijaan"), misspellings ("Pilai") and statuses (arrested / absconding).
  8. Ground truth. Every generated record id (e.g. kyc:<acct>, sub:<phone>, fir:<case>:<name>) is mapped to the true person key.

C2. Step 2: ingestion (jaal/ingest.py)

Goal: turn every source into uniform Python records, each carrying provenance, meaning where it came from.

2.1 Data classes

  • Evidence(doc, loc, text): for example ("cdr.csv", "L214", "<the raw CSV line>"). This is the foundation of explainability. Every later claim points to one of these.
  • PersonRecord: rid, source, name, aliases, phones, dob, age, id_type, id_number, city, evidence, meta. One per person mention in any source.
  • Dataset: holds docs (with SHA-256 and all lines), persons, calls, txns, accounts, vehicles, anpr, handles, messages and cases.

2.2 Normalisers

norm_phone: strip non-digits; if 12 digits starting with 91, drop "91"; if 11 digits starting with 0, drop "0"; accept only 10 digits starting with 6–9. norm_vehicle: upper-case and keep only letters and digits ("AP 28 CX 9911" → "AP28CX9911").

The parser walks the FIR line by line, keeping the line number:

  • A line starting with Complainant: becomes a complainant record. Officer titles (SI, PSI, Inspector…) are skipped, because they are not persons of interest.
  • A numbered line containing arrested / absconding / bailed / notice issued becomes an accused record. The text before the first comma is split on @ or alias into name and aliases. Age comes from aged (about) NN, city from r/o place, City, and status from the bracket.
  • Every line is also scanned for phones, accounts and vehicles, which become case mentions.
  • A modus-operandi lexicon (e.g. "kyc" → KYC-update pretext, "ganja" → narcotics, "renting" → account renting) tags each case.

The subscriber registry, bank KYC (individuals become person records; companies become a "signatory" person record plus an organisation), RTO and social handles all become PersonRecords with evidence = their own CSV line. CDR, transfers, ANPR and messages become event lists that keep their line numbers. manifest() lists every file with its SHA-256, which serves as the chain of custody.

C3. Step 3: entity resolution (jaal/resolve.py)

3.1 canon(name)

Lower-case, replace punctuation with spaces, drop honorifics, then apply the folding rules in order: aa→a, ee→i, oo→u, ai→e, ei→e, ou→u, ph→f, w→v, kh→k, gh→g, th→t, dh→d, bh→b, sh→s, z→j, q→k, ck→k, y→i. Collapse doubled letters, and drop a trailing "e" on tokens longer than 3 letters. Results: "Imraan Shaikh" → [imran, sek]; "Md. Imran Sheikh" → [imran, sek]; "Suresh Pilai" and "SURESH PILLAI" → [sures, pil].

3.2 name_similarity

For full names: 0.4 × JW(first tokens) + 0.6 × JW(last tokens). The surname is weighted more because first names are commonly shared. An initial matches a token starting with the same letter (0.9), so "R. MORE" ~ "Ritu More" gives 0.96. If either side is a single token (an alias like "Rammy"), compare it against every token, scaled ×0.75 unless both are single tokens.

3.3 score_pair and resolve

Score every pair of records (about 21,000 pairs takes well under a second) using the evidence table in B2, and keep the human-readable reasons. Skip registry-vs-registry pairs. Apply stored human decisions: "reject" means never merge; "accept" means always merge. Union-find merges pairs scoring ≥ 0.95. Pairs from 0.65 to 0.95 go into the review queue, de-duplicated to one item per pair of resulting entities.

Building entities: for each union-find group, the display name is chosen by a weighted vote among full names (subscriber 1.2, RTO 1.1, KYC 1.0, FIR 0.8), so "Suresh Pillai" beats the misspelling "Suresh Pilai". The entity id is P:<slug> (e.g. P:vikram-rathod), with a numeric suffix if needed. Aliases, phones, DOB, city and IDs are gathered from all records.

Checkpoint: 208 records → 82 entities, 188 automatic merges, 4 review items; pairwise precision 1.0 and recall 0.936 against ground truth.

C5. Step 5: the analysis engine (jaal/engine.py)

This is the core of the system. Engine.build() runs the whole pipeline. The design rule is rebuild everything (about 0.5 s) after any change, such as a new FIR or a review decision. This is simpler and safer than incremental updates, and the results are always consistent.

build():  load() → resolve() → _build_kg() → _build_person_graph() → _analyse()
_analyse(): PPR → Louvain → _benign_hubs → _probable_victims → NOI → _money → _structural

5.1 Knowledge-graph primitives

node(id, type, label, **attrs) creates or updates a node. edge(u, v, type, evidence, directed) aggregates: calling it again for the same (u, v, type) increments count and weight and appends evidence (capped at 25 rows; the total count is always kept). Node ids carry their type: P: person, PH: phone, AC: account, ORG:, VH:, CASE:, LOC:, SM: handle, IMEI:, GRP:, U: unattributed phone.

5.2 _build_kg in order

  1. Person nodes from entities. For each record, add an ownership edge: KYC → HOLDS account; signatory → CONTROLS org, and org HOLDS account; RTO → OWNS vehicle; social → OPERATES handle; FIR → NAMED_IN case (with role and status). Phone claims are collected from every record.
  2. Handsets: group CDR rows by caller IMEI, then by SIM. IMEIs used by more than one SIM are "shared".
  3. Phone ownership: the subscriber-registry owner wins; otherwise the first claim.
  4. Ghost (fake identity) inference: for each shared IMEI, if exactly one owner is "real" (entity seen in more than the telecom registry) and another owner is registry-only, the second is a ghost. Its SIM is re-attributed to the real person (INFERRED_USER, confidence 0.85), with a reason sentence and evidence from the slip calls.
  5. Phones with REGISTERED_TO / USES edges; IMEI nodes with USED_IN edges.
  6. Calls → CALLED edges (aggregated, with duration, first and last), plus a daily call counter per phone for temporal analysis.
  7. Transfers → directed TRANSFERRED edges with amount sums.
  8. ANPR → SEEN_AT edges. Co-location: sort each camera's hits by time; any two different vehicles within 600 s form a pair; pairs seen ≥ 2 times get CO_LOCATED edges.
  9. Social → POSTED_IN (to group) and MESSAGED (direct).
  10. Cases → CASE nodes with MENTIONS edges to every phone, account and vehicle the FIR text contains.
  11. Phones with no owner get a placeholder U: actor so they still appear in the person network.

5.3 _build_person_graph (projection)

First compute each person's assets (phones, accounts including those of companies they control, vehicles, handles). Then convert each artefact-level edge into a person–person link:

5.4 Who to focus on: NOI, benign hubs, probable victims

  • Seeds = all FIR-accused people. PPR from the seeds = case proximity.
  • Benign hub: degree ≥ max(8, 3× median degree) AND mean tie weight < the median tie weight AND local clustering < 0.15 AND not accused. (Clustering = the fraction of a node's neighbour pairs that are themselves connected.)
  • Probable victim: paid ≥ Rs 50,000 to one accused person (at most 2 such payments, a single payee), the two have ≤ 6 calls and no other channel, no money came back from that accused, and the payer controls no company.
  • NOI = accused ∪ top 30 by PPR (excluding hubs, victims and complainants). After the alerts run, anyone with a severity-3 alert is added and the structural metrics are recomputed.
  • _structural: weighted betweenness, eigenvector and disruption (efficiency loss with the original denominator), all computed inside the NOI subgraph.

Every alert stores its entities, their labels and up to 8 evidence rows. Each person's flag total is the sum of the severities of their alerts.

5.6 Score (_score) and roles (_roles)

priority = 0.25·norm(PPR) + 0.25·norm(betweenness) + 0.15·norm(eigenvector) + 0.20·norm(disruption) + 0.15·min(1, flags/6)

For each term, JAAL also stores its rank and an explanation sentence. Roles are assigned by the first matching rule: complainant-only → Victim; probable victim → Probable victim; controls an org and moves ≥ Rs 5 lakh → Financial controller; rank ≤ 5, never accused, ≥ 3 accused within 2 hops → Suspected orchestrator; ≥ Rs 2 lakh in from ≥ 2 sources with ≥ 60% forwarded → Money mule; betweenness rank ≤ 8 and neighbours in ≥ 2 communities and in NOI → Broker; accused → Field operative; benign hub → Benign hub; in NOI → Associate; otherwise Peripheral.

5.7 Cross-case (_cross_case)

  1. Case set = accused + owners of phones, accounts and vehicles mentioned in the FIR (complainants excluded).
  2. If the two sets share a person: hops = 0, structural = 1.
  3. Otherwise run BFS between every pair of members, twice: once allowing only NOI intermediaries, and once allowing anyone except hubs and victims. Keep the path with the best _path_strength = 1/len(path) × 0.5 per non-NOI intermediary × Π min(1, ln(1+w)/ln 11).
  4. MO Jaccard = |shared tags| / |all tags|. Text similarity = TF-IDF cosine of the "Brief facts" sections.
  5. score = 0.75·structural + 0.15·MO + 0.10·text → strong (≥ 0.45) / moderate (≥ 0.25) / weak. Each hop is explained as "A –[calls ×78]→ B".

5.8 Hidden links, key players, query API, mutations, evaluation

  • _predicted_links: Adamic–Adar on the NOI subgraph; top 12 with their shared contacts.
  • _key_player_set: greedy search for 3 people, each time removing whoever lowers NOI efficiency most; reports the efficiency left and the number of fragments.
  • Read API: summary, person_graph, kg(focus, depth), dossier, timeline, path, targets, document, search.
  • Mutations: ingest_fir(text) saves the file to data/raw/firs and rebuilds; decide(key, accept|reject) saves er_decisions.json and rebuilds.
  • evaluate(): pairwise ER precision and recall; kingpin and decoy ranks for JAAL vs. degree vs. betweenness; precision@12; burners attributed; cross-case correctness; predicted vs. true roles.
Checkpoint: python3 run.py --evaluate shows kingpin_rank.jaal_priority = 2, degree_only = 42, precision_at_k.jaal = 0.833, cross_case_correct = "10/10".

C6. Step 6: audit log, roles and masking (jaal/audit.py)

  • Entry = {seq, ts, user, role, action, target, outcome, detail, prev}. Then hash = SHA-256(JSON of the entry without "hash", keys sorted, compact separators). The first entry's prev is 64 zeros.
  • append holds a thread lock, reads the last line to get prev, and writes one JSON line. verify() walks the file and reports {ok, entries, head} or {ok: false, broken_at, reason}, distinguishing "content modified" from "entry removed or re-ordered".
  • Roles map to permission sets: viewer {graph, targets, alerts, cases, summary}; analyst adds entity, doc, path, ingest, er_read, links, timeline, search; supervisor adds er_decide, audit, evaluate; auditor {summary, audit}.
  • Masking for viewers: phones → 98XXXXXX57, 11–16 digit accounts → XXXXXXXX7812, vehicle plates partly hidden. Ids like PH:9820… become stable opaque tokens PH:#<sha1[:10]>, so the graph still links correctly.

8.2 The graph engine (class Graph in app.js), written by hand

  • Physics per tick: every pair of nodes repels with force k/d² (k = 2400, skipped beyond 500 px); every edge acts as a spring toward rest length L·(1.4 − 0.7w), where w is the log-normalised weight; a weak pull toward the centre; velocity damping ×0.6; and a "temperature" alpha that cools ×0.985 per tick.
  • Pre-settling: when data is loaded, ticks run synchronously (up to 700 ms) until alpha < 0.03, then the view fits to the screen. This makes the layout independent of animation frames, which browsers throttle in background tabs. This fixed a real bug.

E2. Questions you will be asked, with answers

QuestionAnswer
Why not just use Neo4j / networkx?We could, and for state scale we would. For this prototype, pure Python means zero installation, offline use, and every algorithm can be read and explained line by line.
Is this "AI"?It is analytical AI: graph machine learning (PageRank, community detection, link prediction), probabilistic record linkage and anomaly detection. We deliberately avoided opaque deep models so every output is explainable and auditable, which policing requires.
How do you know it works?We planted a known syndicate and compared against the hidden truth: kingpin #2 vs. #42 by degree, precision@12 of 0.83 vs. 0.17, 10/10 case pairs, ER F1 0.97 with no wrong merges.
Isn't the data made up?Yes, on purpose: real data is confidential, and with synthetic data we know the right answer. It is realistic (inconsistent spellings, 7 phone formats, decoys, namesakes). The next step is testing on closed real cases.
What if it accuses an innocent person?It produces ranked leads, not accusations. False-positive controls (benign hubs, victims, human ER review), evidence on every claim, and the audit log keep humans responsible for decisions.
How is the score calculated?0.25 case proximity + 0.25 brokerage + 0.15 influence + 0.20 disruption + 0.15 red flags, each normalised to 0–1. The weights are published and adjustable.
Why is the lieutenant #1, not the kingpin?He is accused, has 4 co-accused and real brokerage, so he scores high. The system still singles out the kingpin as the only top person never named in any FIR, and the role engine flags exactly that pattern.
How does burner detection work?CDRs include the handset IMEI. If a SIM registered to an identity that exists nowhere else shares a handset with a known person's SIM, that identity is likely fake and the SIM is attributed to the known person, with the proving call rows shown.
How do you handle different spellings?Transliteration folding (Shaikh/Sheikh → sek), Jaro–Winkler similarity, initials, aliases, combined with hard evidence such as shared phone, ID or DOB, and a human queue for uncertain pairs.
Can someone edit the logs?They can edit the file, but each entry contains the previous entry's SHA-256, so verification pinpoints the first tampered entry. A test proves this.
Privacy?Role-based access enforced on the server, identifier masking for low roles, every access logged (including denials), and it runs locally so no data leaves the machine. Production needs SSO, encryption and DPDP Act compliance.
How fast is it?Full rebuild in about 0.5 s for 78 people, 6,342 calls and 460 transfers. Exact algorithms scale to a few thousand people; beyond that, use sampling and a graph database.
What about Hindi FIRs?The current parser is regex-based for English. The roadmap is a multilingual NER model (e.g. fine-tuned IndicBERT) that outputs the same records with provenance spans.
Why personalised PageRank?Global importance finds popular people. We want people important to this case, so the random walk restarts at the accused.
What is the network of interest?The accused plus the 30 people suspicion flows to most, minus hubs and victims, plus anyone with severe alerts. Structure is measured inside it.
What does "disruption" mean?How much the network's ability to communicate (global efficiency) drops if this person is removed. The key-player set finds the best 3 to remove together.
How are cases linked?Shared people or artefacts, or a short path through suspects, plus overlapping modus operandi and similar FIR wording, with penalties for weak or civilian-only links.
What is structuring?Splitting money into amounts just below a reporting threshold (here Rs 50,000) to avoid scrutiny. We flag 3 or more such transfers from one account.
What is a mule account?An account rented to criminals: money comes in from several victims and leaves within hours. We flag ≥ 70% outflow within 48 h from ≥ 2 sources.
Is it court-admissible?The tool is not evidence, but it points to admissible records (exact file, line and SHA-256), which supports chain of custody and lets an expert reproduce every number.
Can it learn?Supervisor decisions on identity matches are stored and replayed. Future work: learn the score weights from confirmed case outcomes.
What would you add with more time?Real-data validation, multilingual NER, temporal graph playback, SSO/2FA, a graph database backend, and learned weights.
Why these weights?Chosen to balance case proximity and structural power equally, with disruption next. They are exposed in one place (WEIGHTS) for investigators to adjust.
What's unique compared with i2 or Palantir?Fully explainable and auditable, free, offline, and it includes India-specific handling (transliteration, FIR parsing, UPI/IMPS structuring patterns) plus explicit false-positive controls.
How long did it take to build?(Answer honestly for your team.) About 3,600 lines across 12 files.

44def jaro_winkler(a: str, b: str) -> float:
45 if a == b:
46 return 1.0
47 la, lb = len(a), len(b)
48 if not la or not lb:
49 return 0.0
50 rng = max(la, lb) // 2 - 1
51 ma, mb = [False] * la, [False] * lb
52 m = 0
53 for i, c in enumerate(a):
54 for j in range(max(0, i - rng), min(lb, i + rng + 1)):
55 if not mb[j] and b[j] == c:
56 ma[i] = mb[j] = True
57 m += 1
58 break
59 if not m:
60 return 0.0
61 t, k = 0, 0
62 for i in range(la):
63 if ma[i]:
64 while not mb[k]:
65 k += 1
66 if a[i] != b[k]:
67 t += 1
68 k += 1
69 j = (m / la + m / lb + (m - t / 2) / m) / 3
70 p = 0
71 for x, y in zip(a[:4], b[:4]):
72 if x != y:
73 break
74 p += 1
75 return j + p * 0.1 * (1 - j)

17def betweenness(adj: dict, weighted: bool = False) -> dict:
18 """Brandes (2001) exact betweenness, normalised to [0,1].
19 weighted=True uses Dijkstra with tie_cost(), so brokerage over strong ties counts more."""
20 import heapq
21 bc = dict.fromkeys(adj, 0.0)
22 for s in adj:
23 stack, pred = [], {v: [] for v in adj}
24 sigma = dict.fromkeys(adj, 0)
25 sigma[s] = 1

27 dist = dict.fromkeys(adj, -1)
28 dist[s] = 0
29 q = deque([s])
30 while q:
31 v = q.popleft()
32 stack.append(v)
33 for w in adj[v]:
34 if dist[w] < 0:
35 dist[w] = dist[v] + 1
36 q.append(w)
37 if dist[w] == dist[v] + 1:
38 sigma[w] += sigma[v]
39 pred[w].append(v)
40 else:
41 dist, settled, c = {s: 0.0}, set(), 1
42 pq = [(0.0, 0, s)]

55 heapq.heappush(pq, (nd, c, w))

279 def link(p1, p2, channel, w, kg_edge, **extra):

285 e = self.pg_edges.setdefault(key, {"u": key[0], "v": key[1], "weight": 0.0, "channels": Counter(),
286 "kg_edges": [], "amount": 0})
287 e["weight"] += w
288 e["channels"][channel] += extra.get("n", 1)
289 e["amount"] += extra.get("amount", 0)
290 if kg_edge and kg_edge["id"] not in e["kg_edges"]:

301 elif t == "MESSAGED":

368 def _structural(self):
369 adj, ppl = self.adj, self.person_ids
370 sub = {p: {q: w for q, w in adj[p].items() if q in self.noi} for p in self.noi}
371 self.noi_adj = sub

374 self.ev = {p: ev_noi.get(p, 0.0) for p in ppl}

408 def _benign_hubs(self):
409 """High-degree actors whose ties are many, weak and mutually unconnected (delivery agents,
410 call centres, shopkeepers). Kept in the graph, but excluded from the network of interest."""
411 adj = self.adj
412 degs = sorted(self.deg.values())
413 med_deg = degs[len(degs) // 2] or 1
414 ties = sorted(w for p in adj for w in adj[p].values())
415 med_tie = ties[len(ties) // 2] if ties else 1
416 self.hub_stats, self.benign_hubs = {}, set()
417 for p in self.person_ids:
418 nb = list(adj[p])
419 k = len(nb)
420 if k < max(8, 3 * med_deg):
421 continue
422 links = sum(1 for i, a in enumerate(nb) for b in nb[i + 1:] if b in adj[a])
423 clustering = 2 * links / (k * (k - 1))
424 mean_tie = sum(adj[p].values()) / k
425 self.hub_stats[p] = {"degree": k, "median_degree": med_deg, "mean_tie": round(mean_tie, 1),
426 "median_tie": med_tie, "clustering": round(clustering, 3)}
427 if mean_tie < med_tie and clustering < 0.15 and p not in self.accused:
428 self.benign_hubs.add(p)

698 def _tfidf(self):
699 docs = {c: re.findall(r"[a-z]{3,}", self.ds.cases[c]["text"].split("Brief facts")[-1].lower()) for c in self.ds.cases}
700 df = Counter(w for ws in docs.values() for w in set(ws))
701 N = len(docs)
702 vec = {}
703 for c, ws in docs.items():
704 tf = Counter(ws)
705 v = {w: (n / len(ws)) * math.log((1 + N) / (1 + df[w])) for w, n in tf.items()}
706 norm = math.sqrt(sum(x * x for x in v.values())) or 1
707 vec[c] = {w: x / norm for w, x in v.items()}

710 def _cross_case(self):
711 sets = self._case_sets()
712 self.case_sets = sets
713 vec = self._tfidf()
714 cases = sorted(sets)
715 self.case_links = []
716 for i, a in enumerate(cases):
717 for b in cases[i + 1:]:
718 shared = set(sets[a]) & set(sets[b])
719 best = None
720 if shared:
721 p = sorted(shared)[0]
722 best = {"hops": 0, "path": [p]}
723 else:
724 outside = {p for p in self.adj if p not in self.noi} | self.victims
725 for s in sets[a]:
726 for t in sets[b]:
727 if s not in self.adj or t not in self.adj:
728 continue
729 for banned in (self.benign_hubs | outside, self.benign_hubs | self.victims):
730 path = self._bfs_path(s, t, banned=banned)
731 if path:
732 sc = self._path_strength(path)
733 if best is None or sc > best["structural"]:
734 best = {"hops": len(path) - 1, "path": path, "structural": sc}
735 structural = 0 if not best else best.get("structural", 1.0)
736 mo_a, mo_b = set(self.ds.cases[a]["mo"]), set(self.ds.cases[b]["mo"])
737 mo_j = len(mo_a & mo_b) / len(mo_a | mo_b) if mo_a | mo_b else 0
738 cos = sum(x * vec[b].get(w, 0) for w, x in vec[a].items())
739 score = round(0.75 * structural + 0.15 * mo_j + 0.10 * cos, 3)
740 strength = "strong" if score >= 0.45 else "moderate" if score >= 0.25 else "weak"
741 hops_txt = []
742 if best:
743 for x, y in zip(best["path"], best["path"][1:]):
744 hops_txt.append(self._hop_explain(x, y))
745 self.case_links.append({
746 "a": a, "b": b, "score": score, "strength": strength, "structural": round(structural, 3),
747 "mo_overlap": sorted(mo_a & mo_b), "text_similarity": round(cos, 3),
748 "hops": best["hops"] if best else None, "path": best["path"] if best else [],
749 "path_labels": [self.nodes[x]["label"] for x in (best["path"] if best else [])],
750 "explain": ((f"{self.nodes[best['path'][0]]['label']} appears in both cases "
751 f"({sets[a][best['path'][0]]} / {sets[b][best['path'][0]]})." if best and best["hops"] == 0
752 else " → ".join(hops_txt)) if best else "No connecting path."),
753 "excluded": [self.nodes[h]["label"] for h in sorted(self.benign_hubs | self.victims)]})

756 def _path_strength(self, path):
757 """1/(1+hops); halved for every intermediary outside the network of interest; each hop is
758 discounted by tie strength (a single message is weak evidence, 10+ interactions is full)."""
759 sc = 1 / (len(path))
760 for x in path[1:-1]:
761 if x not in self.noi:
762 sc *= 0.5
763 for x, y in zip(path, path[1:]):
764 w = self.adj[x][y]
765 sc *= min(1.0, math.log1p(w) / math.log1p(10))
766 return sc

768 def _bfs_path(self, s, t, banned=frozenset()):
769 from collections import deque
770 prev, q = {s: None}, deque([s])
771 while q:
772 v = q.popleft()
773 if v == t:
774 break
775 for w in sorted(self.adj[v]):
776 if w not in prev and (w not in banned or w == t):
777 prev[w] = v
778 q.append(w)
779 if t not in prev:
780 return None
781 path = [t]
782 while prev[path[-1]] is not None:
783 path.append(prev[path[-1]])
784 return path[::-1]

803 def _key_player_set(self, k: int = 3):
804 """Greedy selection of k people whose joint removal most fragments the network of interest."""
805 chosen, base = [], alg.global_efficiency(self.adj, self.noi)
806 cur = set(self.noi)
807 steps = []
808 for _ in range(k):
809 best, best_eff = None, None
810 for p in sorted(cur):
811 eff = alg.global_efficiency(self.adj, cur - {p})
812 if best_eff is None or eff < best_eff:
813 best, best_eff = p, eff
814 chosen.append(best)
815 cur.discard(best)
816 steps.append({"pid": best, "label": self.nodes[best]["label"],
817 "efficiency_left": round(best_eff / base, 3) if base else 0})
818 comps = alg.components(self.adj, cur)
819 self.key_players = {"set": steps, "fragments_after": len(comps),
820 "largest_fragment": max(len(c) for c in comps) if comps else 0, "noi_size": len(self.noi)}
